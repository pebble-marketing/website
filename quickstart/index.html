Quick start

Ripple

For anyone testing or demoing Ripple.

Connect it once, then talk to it in plain English. It answers from your brand instead of guessing.

What Ripple does

Your brand foundation, somewhere your AI can read it

It knows your brand.

Reads your brand rules, voice guide, and channel guides before it writes a word. Opens your positioning, market research, competitor landscape, and buyer personas. Pulls up the same playbooks Pebble uses to produce work in your voice.

It holds your proof.

Every claim about your business comes with a source and a date beside it. It also knows which claims are cleared to publish and which ones are for sales conversations only.

It remembers.

Decisions you've made, corrections you've given, preferences it picked up along the way. What's been published, what's still in flight, and what's waiting on you.

It asks your buyers.

Ripple carries simulated buyers built from your research. You can ask them a question and get each one's answer separately, put a draft in front of them for reactions, or hand them two versions and find out which one lands.

It reviews before anything goes out.

Every draft gets graded against your quality standards. Every number in it gets checked against your records. A draft that reaches for a figure nobody registered comes back marked, with the figure named.

The strategy, end to end

One connected chain

Ripple holds your strategy as one connected chain, and every draft comes out of it in that order.

Your brand is the foundation.

Core messaging, what you claim and the proof behind each claim, how you sound, and the rules about what you'd never say.

Your go-to-market plan sits on top of it.

Who you're going after and how you qualify them, which channels do which job, what a buyer can buy at each stage, and how someone moves from a first symptom to a decision.

Your channels execute it.

Each one carries its own guide: what belongs there, what the format demands, and which part of the go-to-market plan it serves.

That order is why a draft can be on-voice and still be wrong. A post in your exact voice, aimed at the wrong buyer at the wrong stage, is off-strategy. Ripple reads down the whole chain before it writes, and you can walk it yourself with the first three prompts below.

What you can ask it to do

Nothing to memorize and no commands to type. Ask in plain English and Ripple reaches for what it needs.

Read your brand rules and open any document behind them. Pull up your voice examples. Recall past decisions, preferences, and open questions. Look up what's been published. Draft in your voice for any channel you run. Ask your buyers a question, or put a draft in front of them. Send a draft through quality review. Check a claim against your records. Save a piece as a “more like this” or “never again.” Flag something that reads off-brand. Send a question to the Pebble team.

Set up

Before you connect

Ripple authenticates using a GitHub user name. Please send Pebble your user name, not your email, before accessing the system. You will not be able to access Ripple until you are registered.

Ripple is built to use with almost any AI tool that accepts a custom MCP. We have included instructions for setting up with Claude below. If you are using another tool and have questions about set up, please contact Pebble.

Built for Sonnet 5 or similar. Higher models work. Ripple is tuned to be token-efficient.

Connect in Claude

  1. Open Settings → Connectors → Add custom connector.
  2. Name it Ripple, paste in https://ripple-mcp-server.fly.dev/mcp/, click Add.
  3. Click Connect, sign in with your GitHub account, then Authorize.
  4. Open a chat, click the tools menu, and switch Ripple on.

Check it worked

Am I connected to Ripple?

Then:

What do you know about my brand?

The first confirms the connection. The second confirms you landed on the right brand. If the answer to the second could describe any company, Ripple isn't switched on in that chat.

How to use Ripple

Open every session the same way

Be the strategist.

That tells Ripple to load your brand, your plan, and where the engagement stands before it does anything else. Everything after it runs on your material rather than on general knowledge.

Then work through these

Six prompts, in order. Each shows a different part of the system, and each one tells you what a working answer looks like.

1Start with what it knows

What do you know about my brand, how we sound, and who our buyers are?

You should get specifics you recognize. Names, positions, the way the brand talks. Not adjectives.

2Ask about your proof

What stats do we have as proof, and where does each one come from?

You should get a list of claims with a source beside each one, and a date on the ones that need it. The second column is the part that matters. Generic AI hands you a confident number from nowhere. Ripple only reaches for proof someone registered.

What you're seeing is the publishable set. Some claims are cleared for sales conversations and stay behind, so this isn't everything on file.

3Ask about the plan and the channels

What's our go-to-market plan, and what job does each channel do in it?

You should get your real targets, your channels, and what each one is for. Follow it up:

What has to be in a LinkedIn post for us, and what would you never do there?

Every channel carries its own guide. That's why a draft for one channel doesn't read like a draft for another.

4Ask your buyers something

Ask our buyers what they'd search for when they're looking for {EXAMPLE TOPIC}.

Each buyer answers on their own, in their own words.

This is a question, not a review. It carries no verdict.

5Write something

Draft a LinkedIn post about {EXAMPLE TOPIC}. Tell me which channel guide and voice examples you used.

Ask for the sources every time while you're testing. A draft that names the guide and the examples it worked from is drafting from your brand. A draft that can't is writing about it.

6Send it to review

I'm happy with this. Get it reviewed.

This one holds the screen while the review runs. That's expected, not a hang.

You'll get a verdict and written notes. Then Ripple asks whether you also want buyer reactions. That question is part of the process. Answer either way.

A Revise verdict is the system doing its job. If the draft used a number that isn't in your records, the review names the number and won't pass it. The review is automated, it runs against your engagement's quality standards, and it can't be talked out of a verdict.

Close every session

Let's close out. Log what we decided, and remember that we'd never say “{PHRASE}”.

Corrections and decisions stick. Close this way and the next session starts further along than this one did. Close the tab without it and that context is gone.

Good to know

What it won't do

Ripple hands you drafts. It doesn't publish, it doesn't post to your social accounts, and it doesn't send your email. Putting something live is a step you or Pebble take.

It won't see another company's information, and no one else can see yours.
